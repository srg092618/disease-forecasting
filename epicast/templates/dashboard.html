What the chart shows

  • The blue line is the published estimate or count. The orange line is the forecast median: the model thinks the outcome is as likely to be above it as below it.
  • The shaded bands are prediction intervals. The widest is the 95% interval. In the backtest, the final intervals contained the outcome close to 95% of the time; the exact figures are on the Accuracy tab.
  • "Forecast made in" lets you replay a past forecast. The model sees only the data up to that year, and the faded line shows what happened next.

Where the numbers come from

    All sources

      Models

      • Naive: next value equals the last value. The benchmark every other model has to beat.
      • Drift, exponential smoothing, ARIMA: classical time-series models fitted to each country separately.
      • Global gradient boosting: one machine-learning model trained on all countries at once.
      • Renewal equation and Richards curve (outbreaks only): epidemic models built from the reproduction number and from an S-shaped growth curve.
      • Recurrence rate (map outlook): how often new outbreaks have started since 2007, turned into the chance of at least one in the next 12 months. It is a base rate, not a prediction of where or when.
      • Ensemble: the median of the member models at each quantile. Members are fixed in advance.

      What to keep in mind

      • Malaria and HIV figures are themselves model estimates by WHO and UNAIDS, and they are revised every year, including for past years. The backtest scores forecasts against today's version of history, which is easier than forecasting in real time.
      • The Bundibugyo outbreak is a live emergency. Its weekly counts are laboratory confirmations, which lag infections and arrive in batches; the reproduction number assumes the serial interval measured for Ebola virus in West Africa. Treat those forecasts as a modelling exercise and follow WHO and national health authorities for decisions.
      • These are statistical extrapolations. They do not know about funding changes, new vaccines or treatments, conflict, or drug resistance unless those already show in the data.
      • Countries where a disease is nearly eliminated are not forecast: malaria below 1 case per 1,000 at risk, HIV below 1,000 new infections a year (UNAIDS rounds small numbers heavily).
